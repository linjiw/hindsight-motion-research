The information bill

RVQ: 2 × 7 bits × 10 Hz = 140 bit/s. body9 adds 9 × 12 × 10 = 1,080 bit/s. leg12 adds 12 × 12 × 10 = 1,440 bit/s.

All methods additionally retain 11,200 bit/s of root state. body9 + leg12 therefore uses 13,860 bit/s including root, before entry, model, and container overhead. NPZ storage is not a packed codec.

The qualification gate

Pass native traversal checks and retain fidelity to the original reference: mean body error ≤10 cm, maximum root XY error ≤25 cm, and 200 valid control steps.

A pair qualifies only when both continuations pass all three perturbations. Smoothing also changes within-patch motion; leg replacement changes both positions and velocities.

DEPLOYED ACTOR VIEW

What the robot can know now

Measured proprioceptive history, previous actions, available scene observations, goal and optional controls. Future scene tokens must distinguish unknown space from known free space.

TRAINING-ONLY VIEW

What teaches the behavior

Future reference, privileged simulator state, teacher actions or validated motor targets, relation labels, and support masks. Source IDs and future motion codes must not leak into the causal actor.

A tracker provides motor expertise. Task expertise also needs a qualified continuation selector or planner. Teacher labels at student-visited states must be queried at that same state; unsupported recovery is not a positive imitation target.

How to reuse this work in BFM research
  1. Start with the public contracts. Inspect protocols, scoring code, and aggregate summaries. Native data generation requires separately authorized local motion and runtime assets.
  2. Choose a motor interface. Pin teacher checkpoint, joint order, action scale, history layout, and decoder schema. Revalidate targets when the controller changes.
  3. Keep the views separate. Train only on supported rows; keep privileged reference and outcomes out of actor inputs. The latest representation collection has 19,200 control rows and 16,800 supported imitation rows.
  4. Replace one component at a time. Compare a new codec under the frozen geometry and execution scorer, or a new student under the same dataset, decoder, and training budget.
  5. Test independent scene-first tasks. Group descendants by source ancestry. Success on reused motion pairs is development evidence, not held-out navigation generalization.

What “LLM-compatible” would mean

A typed sequence could combine observed scene elements, goals, behavior options, and outcome predictions. Discrete motion codes offer one sequence interface; they do not acquire language meaning automatically.

A future language model may select a goal or constrained behavior. A causal policy and validated motor interface still execute it. Compare structured goals without language against language-conditioned goals.

What scene tokenization would add

Begin with metric object sets: shape, relative pose, dimensions, and observation state. Keep exact collision geometry for checks; a floor heightmap cannot represent overhead clearance at the same XY coordinate.

A world-model extension would predict outcomes of candidate actions, then test calibration against new executions. Neither learned scene tokens nor a world model is implemented here yet.
